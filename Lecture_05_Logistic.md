## Logistic Population Growth

> density independence -  b and d do not depend on N

> Figure 5.1 b and d as a function of N (r is the difference)

![Lec_05.1](/Users/ngotelli/Desktop/githubRepos/BCOR-102-Notes/LectureFigures/Lec_05.1.jpg)

> density dependence - b decreases with N and/or d increases with N

> Figure 5.2 b and d as a function of N (now with density dependence)

![Lec_05.2](/Users/ngotelli/Desktop/githubRepos/BCOR-102-Notes/LectureFigures/Lec_05.2.jpg)


$$
b=b_0-aN
$$

$$
d = d_0 + cN
$$

$$
\frac{dN}{dt}=(b - d)N \mbox{    (density independent)}
$$

$$
\frac{dN}{dt}=((b_0-aN) - (d_0+cN)N \mbox{    (density dependent)}
$$

$$
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN(1-\frac{N}{K})
$$

> K carrying capacity - maximum number of individuals possible in a local population

> unused resources for population growth $(1-\frac{N}{K})$

## When does dN/dt = 0?

$$
r = 0
$$

$$
N = 0
$$

$$
N = K
$$

$$
N \longrightarrow 0 \mbox{ }\frac{dN}{dt}\longrightarrow rN
$$

$$
N \longrightarrow K \mbox{ }\frac{dN}{dt}\longrightarrow 0
$$

## Harvesting and Maximum Yield - "The Tragedy Of The Commons"

> Figure 5.6 logistic growth curve with harvesting strategies at abundances 1, 2, and 3

![Lec_05.6](/Users/ngotelli/Desktop/githubRepos/BCOR-102-Notes/LectureFigures/Lec_05.6.jpg)

$$
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN(1-\frac{N}{K})
$$

Here is an example of harvesting annually from a trout pond

r = 0.1 individuals/individual*year

K = 1000 individuals

### Strategy \#1 - N held close to K

if K = 900 individuals
dN/dt = (0.1)(900)(1 - (900/1000)) = 9 fish per year
low yield because of crowding and low per capita birth and death rates

### Strategy \#2 - N held close to 0

if K = 50 individuals
dN/dt = (0.1)(50)(1 - (50/1000)) = 4.5 fish per year
low yield because of small N

### Strategy \#2 - N held close to 0

if K = 500 individuals
dN/dt = (0.1)(500)(1 - (500/1000)) = 25 fish per year
optimal yield because N = 1/2 of K

lobster fishing as a success story
most natural resources over-exploited

> Tragedy of the Commons - overexploitation of resources by each individual reduces long-term yields

## Assumptions of Logistic Growth Model

1. No I or E
2. No genetic structure
3. No age or size structure
4. Continuous growth with no time lags
5. constant K with linear density dependence
